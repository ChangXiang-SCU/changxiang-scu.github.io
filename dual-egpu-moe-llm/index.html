A 176B MoE on a Handheld, over Two eGPUs

Xiang Chang
Open-source engineering notes · 2026
Code Dense vs. MoE Benchmarks
Figure placeholderstatic/teaser.png

A GPD WIN Max 2 (Ryzen 7 7840U, 64 GB RAM) driving two RX 6950XT eGPUs over OCuLink Gen4 x4.

Overview

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is a 176B mixture-of-experts model with 512 experts per layer. Its experts alone take 46.1 GB, so the 32 GB of VRAM on two RX 6950XT cards can never hold them. This project makes it run anyway, on a GPD WIN Max 2 handheld over OCuLink, on Windows 11 with the llama.cpp Vulkan backend.

With a GPU-resident LRU cache of hot experts, decode reaches 17.8 to 19.6 tok/s at the model's full native 256k context, up from 8.2 tok/s with the same two cards and no cache. The same machine then ran a 27B dense model at 6-bit and beat the MoE on every axis that matters, including 24x faster prefill. That comparison is the most useful result for anyone choosing a model for a 32 GB machine.

Approach

A three-tier memory hierarchy. VRAM holds a copy of the hot experts, not a move: the authoritative weights stay in RAM.

Expert selection is close to uniform in the long run, so statically pinning half of the experts on the GPUs serves about half of the accesses (6.1 tok/s). An LRU cache of similar size serves about 93% of them. What matters is not how much fits, but what share of accesses land on the GPU.

The cache comes from llama.cpp PR #27861: a per-layer LRU cache of expert slices in VRAM. With -sm layer, each GPU caches the experts of the layers it owns. This repository adds two small patches on top (MSVC linking, and letting the cache serve small multi-token batches for speculative decoding), a BIOS fix for running two large-BAR eGPUs at once, and a recipe for building llama.cpp with Vulkan on Windows without the Vulkan SDK.

Prefill is the wall for the MoE: batches larger than the cache limit skip the cache, so all prefill expert compute runs on the Ryzen 7 7840U at about 18 tok/s. Letting prefill use the cache is worth +75%, but it is numerically broken above batch 16, so it was reported upstream rather than shipped. Every configuration was checked with greedy decoding against output hashes.
